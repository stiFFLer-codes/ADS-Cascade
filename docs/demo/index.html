02 · The insight that makes it cheap

Most of it is repetition, not judgment.

Phase 1 measured it: within a company, the same product is booked to the same account almost every time. So the system doesn't need to "think" about most lines — it needs to remember. The product catalog is the brain; an AI model is a tail worker for the few genuinely new cases.

Under the hood — what "determinism" means
We score each product by its Accounting Determinism Score (ADS) = the share of the dominant account across all times that product was booked. ADS 1.0 = always the same account; 0 = spread evenly. The weighted dataset ADS is 0.847; unweighted 0.964. The gap means the hardest products are also the highest-volume — the core challenge Phase 2 is built around. Everything here is reproducible from data/outputs/.

05 · Phase 2 — the confidence cascade

Try the cheap, certain path first. Escalate only what's uncertain.

Every line runs a fixed order: the company's own precedent, then shared patterns, then fuzzy retrieval, then — rarely — an LLM. Where it lands sets one of four tiers.

Tier 1 · deterministic → auto-apply Tier 2 · strong match → auto + spot-check Tier 3 · uncertain → human review Tier 4 · never seen → classify once, learns forever

LLM re-ranking uses Groq (llama-3.3-70b) as a free stand-in, behind a model-agnostic adapter — throttled to ~27 of the 30 RPM free-tier limit, with responses cached. In production this is one config swap to Claude Haiku; nothing else changes. The key lives in a git-ignored .env.

10 · Company context — the CAEN code

CAEN is the cold-start prior.

Every company in the manifest carries a CAEN code — its industry classification — next to its CUI and filings. We capture it as company context and, honestly, we don't use it as a classification feature yet.

Its designed role is the cold-start prior: a brand-new client with no history can borrow patterns from companies in the same industry — a bakery books like other bakeries — until its own precedent accumulates. It's a documented scaling lever, refreshed monthly from the ANAF registry.

manifest record

{
  "company_name": "...",
  "cui": "RO90099099",
  "caen_code": "5610",   ← industry
  "xml_file_url": "...",
  "created_at": "..."
}

Same CAEN → similar product mix → a sensible first guess before the company has any history of its own.

11 · Why it works & how it scales

Measured, not asserted.

Read honestly: 98.4% is Tier-1 accuracy — the confident slice (42.8% of lines), not a system-wide number. Raw pre-review accuracy across all tiers is ~67%; by design the system auto-applies only what it's sure of and routes the rest to a human, rather than booking everything. And this hold-out was a random split — a temporal split (train on earlier invoices, test on later ones) is the honest test of forward generalisation, and is a stated next step.

It scales because the expensive part barely runs. Cost tracks the AI tail, and the tail shrinks as corrections convert products to deterministic rules. Each company gets its own knowledge base; the model sits behind a swappable adapter.

Scaling levers

  • per-company KBs — no cross-tenant bleed
  • corrections shrink the review tail over time
  • CAEN prior warms up new clients faster
  • model-agnostic — Groq today, Haiku tomorrow
  • cached, event-driven — no mass reprocessing
12 · Stated plainly

What this prototype is — and isn't.

It's a validated demo

10 receipts + Phase 1 data. OCR quality here is qualitative, not a statistical benchmark — a larger labelled set comes next.

Cold-start is real

A new client reviews more at first. That's deliberate — the alternative is silent errors in the books. It self-corrects as precedent grows.

Production pieces are parked

WhatsApp intake, the review UI, and ERP export await business decisions — logged in OPEN_QUESTIONS.md, not guessed.

Reproducibility — did this actually run?
Yes. Every number on this page comes from committed artifacts in data/outputs/phase2/. Textract and LLM responses are cached, so the whole pipeline reproduces offline, with no keys and $0, and gives identical results. The scripts are in scripts/phase2/; how to run them is in STATE.md.
